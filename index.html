Abstract

Most progress metrics for physical AI still lean on scripted lab tasks or short egocentric clips that do not look like day-to-day skilled work. BlueCollar-Bench is a multi-lab effort to build a shared evaluation and an open egocentric dataset centered on real work cycles: the same kinds of tasks, repeated across people, stations, sites, and countries.

The scientific goal is a durable community resource for asking how models predict what happens next, anticipate failure, and generalize when the worker, the station, or the environment changes. Exact tracks, metrics, and splits are being settled with the collaborator group before evaluation begins.

Overview

The proposal starts from industrial and other skilled physical settings, but the organizing idea under discussion is the structure of the data: many repeated executions of the same work, across workers and places. That structure is what lets a benchmark ask about repetition, execution quality, and generalization in ways that short curated clips usually cannot.

Open questions the group is working through include what the benchmark should be known for, which task families belong in a first release, whether repetition and quality should cut across every track, and how hold-outs (new worker, station, site, country, industry) should separate task novelty from environment novelty. A planning freeze comes before any public scores, so definitions do not move once evaluation starts. Target venue: ICML 2027.

Dataset

Alongside the benchmark, the project plans a joint release of a large open egocentric dataset of skilled physical work. The broader corpus is built for diversity across people and capture settings; the public split and evaluation protocol will be fixed with collaborators. The benchmark itself will sit on a subset of that data.
